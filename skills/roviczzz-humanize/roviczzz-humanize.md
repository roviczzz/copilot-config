# Skill: Academic Text Humanization & De-Sloping

This skill provides rules, strategies, and audits to paraphrase academic texts (such as theses and research papers) to remove machine-generated patterns ("slop") while preserving a professional, academic tone.

---

## 1. Core Guidelines

To produce human-grade academic writing, you must strip away AI-generated tells and focus on direct, human-driven actions. The following rules must be applied strictly and without exception.

### Rule 1: Zero Adverbs
Absolutely no adverbs are allowed in the final text. This includes:
*   Manner adverbs ending in `-ly` (e.g., *successfully, effectively, accurately, efficiently, highly, seamlessly, simultaneously, easily, consistently, specifically, generally*).
*   Temporal/conjunctive adverbs (e.g., *then, also, now, still, currently, overall, additionally, finally, however, nevertheless*).
*   To modify verbs or adjectives, use structural adjectives or separate, direct sentences.

### Rule 2: Zero Dashes & Hyphens
Punctuation hyphens, en-dashes, and em-dashes are banned.
*   Convert hyphenated adjectives into multi-word descriptions (e.g., change *e-commerce* to *electronic commerce*; change *user-friendly* to *simple* or *accessible*; change *four-point* to *four point*; change *long-term* to *long term*).
*   Resolve table or section hyphens into clean spacing (e.g., change *Table 2 – A* to *Table 2 A*).

### Rule 3: The Rule of Two (No Lists of Three)
No sentence may contain a list of three or more items.
*   If a source text lists three or more variables, features, or metrics, split the list into pairs or distribute them across multiple sentences.
*   *Example*: Instead of *"We assessed Usability, Reliability, and Security,"* write: *"We assessed Usability and Reliability. Proponents studied Security."*

### Rule 4: Active Voice with Human Subjects
Abstract, inanimate concepts must never act as the subjects of active verbs.
*   **Incorrect (Abstract Agency)**: *"The evaluation confirms that..."* or *"This test demonstrates..."*
*   **Correct (Human Agency)**: *"Researchers confirm that..."* or *"Through this test, proponents demonstrated..."*
*   Ensure sentences are led by concrete human actors (e.g., *proponents, researchers, developers, managers, respondents, users*).

### Rule 5: Restricted Academic Terminology
*   **Banned Words**: Do not use the terms *project members* or *investigators*.
*   **Allowed Words**: Use *only* the terms **proponents** or **researchers** to refer to the authors of the study.

### Rule 6: Varied Sentence & Paragraph Openings
Do not start consecutive sentences or paragraphs with the same words (specifically avoid starting paragraphs with the definite article *"The"*).
*   Vary paragraph openings using prepositions (*"Through..."*, *"Regarding..."*, *"In..."*), gerunds (*"Using..."*, *"By..."*), or active human nouns (*"Researchers..."*, *"Proponents..."*).

---

## 2. The Four-Part Output Template

When humanizing and editing text, structure your output into these four parts:

### Part 1: Draft Rewrite
Present the initial humanized draft of the text following all core guidelines.

### Part 2: AI Audit
Explain why the original text was flagged as AI-generated. Highlight specific AI-tells (such as abstract agency, adverbs, hyphenation, and triple lists) that were removed.

### Part 3: Final Polished Rewrite
Provide the final, fully polished version of the text, verifying that it contains:
1. Zero adverbs.
2. Zero hyphens or dashes.
3. No lists of three or more items.
4. Correct human-led active voice.
5. Unique paragraph opening styles.

### Part 4: Summary of Changes
Provide a brief summary detailing exactly how the guidelines were met (e.g., how adverbs were removed, how lists of three were split, and how human agency was restored).
